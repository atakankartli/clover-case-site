How I built a Kanoodle-style ball puzzle game for iPhone

November 7, 2026 · Atakan, solo developer of Clover Case

I loved those ball-piece packing puzzles as a kid: a little case, a handful of plastic pieces made of fused balls, and a picture card showing which pieces start on the board. Kanoodle is the one most people know. I wanted that same feeling on my phone, and I couldn't find an app that got it right, so I made one. It's called Clover Case, and it's free on the App Store.

12 pieces, 55 balls

The classic board is 11 by 5 holes. The piece set is twelve shapes: one of 3 balls, three of 4 and eight of 5, which adds up to exactly 55 balls. If every piece goes in, the board is full, and there's no slack anywhere. That's what makes this kind of puzzle so satisfying. The last piece either fits perfectly or it doesn't fit at all.

Making levels that are actually solvable

A level is a board with some pieces already locked in place, like the picture cards in the physical game. I wrote a solver that packs the full board, then removes pieces to leave a puzzle with a known answer. The fewer pieces you're given, the harder it gets. That gives four difficulty books, 40 levels each, so 160 in total. Each level is generated from a fixed seed, so "Medium 7" is the same puzzle on every phone. That means you can compare notes with a friend.

Beyond the rectangle: triangle and hexagon cases

Once the classic board felt good, I added a triangle case (51 holes) and a hexagon case (53 holes). The hexagon is the nastiest. It has six corners and no straight edge to lean against, which kills the "fill the wall first" habit most players rely on.

The daily puzzle

Every day there's one empty-board puzzle that's the same for everyone in the world, with a leaderboard. It rolls over at midnight UTC. It has quickly become the part I play most myself.

Rendering and feel

The board and pieces are real 3D (Three.js) inside a small Expo app. Most of the work wasn't the 3D, though. It was the feel: how a piece lifts when you grab it, how it rotates and flips with one finger, and how it snaps when it lands. The whole thing sits inside a pixel-art travel case with a clover on the lid, hence the name.
